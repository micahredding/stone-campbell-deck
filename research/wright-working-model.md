# The church as working model of new creation: Wright and Stone-Campbell, side by side

N. T. Wright argues that the church is "the small working model of new creation"; that its substance is unity; that unity has to be visible; that a visibly united, many-coloured church is the sign to the principalities and powers (Eph 3:10); and that it draws the world. Below, his language is set beside the matching language of Thomas and Alexander Campbell, Barton Stone, the *Last Will and Testament*, and Cane Ridge. Most practices (singing, gathering, scripture, the table) are sub-points under the step they serve; a closing Practices section holds the Supper as foretaste and the one loaf.

Wright's sources: *God's Homecoming* (HarperOne, 2025), especially ch. 13, "The Polychrome Church as the Sign to the Powers"; *The Vision of Ephesians* (SPCK, 2025); *Simply Christian* (HarperOne, 2006). The full research notes, with every quotation in context, are in the class research database (`10 Research Projects/The Church as Working Model (Wright)/`).

<summary>4d. "Drab uniformity": the institutional church as the powers' imitator</summary>


| Wright | Stone-Campbell |
|---|---|
| "The principalities and powers always tend toward a monochrome, drab uniformity, squashing everything into the same shape and dealing violently with the bits that refuse to conform. But God's creation isn't like that, and his new creation isn't supposed to be like that either." (GH p. 276) | "Unity of opinion, expressed in subscription to voluminous dogmas imported from Geneva, Westminster, Edinburgh, or Rome, is made the bond of union; and **a difference in the tenth or ten-thousandth shade of opinion frequently becomes the actual cause of dismemberment or expulsion**." (CS p. 123) |
| "The community of which Paul speaks is precisely polychrome." (GH p. 276) | "To establish what is called a system of orthodox opinions as the bond of union was, in fact, offering a premium for new diversities in opinion… a love regulated by similarity of opinion is only a love to one's own opinion." (CS p. 121) |
| | That judgment "is an express violation of the law of Christ, **a daring usurpation of his throne**, and a gross intrusion upon the rights and liberties of his subjects." (D&A, Address) |
| | "God loves his children more than our creeds, and man was not made for the Bible, but the Bible for man." (A. Campbell, c. 1810, in Richardson, *Memoirs* I:353–354) |

*This application is ours.* Wright's "polychrome" is mainly ethnic and cultural; the Rom 14 line in 4c is his own bridge to differences of practice and scruple.

|---|---|

(GH p. 277) | Methodist, Baptist and Presbyterian preachers together at Cane Ridge (Stone, Bio., p. 37). Stone calls the "singing exercise… most heavenly" (Bio., pp. 41–42). |
| "One mind and one mouth; that is Paul's intention." (GH p. 272, Rom 15:5–6) | "…whether we shall **sing from book or from memory, prose or verse**, &c. &c. are matters in which our conceptions of expediency, decency, and good order may have free scope." (CS pp. 78–79) |

*Irony for class:* Campbell treated *how* to sing as a matter of "free scope." His heirs made the instrument a test of fellowship, and the singing Wright reads as unity's sign became a line of division.

<summary>4g. Scripture: a meeting place, not a weapon</summary>


| Wright | Stone-Campbell |
|---|---|
| "Supposing scripture, like the sacraments, is one of the points where heaven and earth overlap and interlock?" (SC p. 181) | "All the means of grace are, therefore, only the means of impressing this seal upon the heart… Testimony and faith are but the channel through which these facts, or the hand of God, draws the image on the heart and character of man." (CS pp. 116–117) |
| "Reading scripture, like praying and sharing in the sacraments, is one of the means by which the life of heaven and the life of earth interlock… places to go where God has promised to meet with his people." (SC p. 188) | "We will, that the people henceforth take the Bible as the only sure guide to heaven." (LWT, 1804) |
| "The Bible, in fact, is not simply an authoritative description of a saving plan, as though it were just an aerial photograph… It is part of the saving plan itself." (SC pp. 185–186) | "The New Testament was not designed to occupy the same place in theological seminaries that the carcasses of malefactors are condemned to occupy in medical halls… Christianity consists infinitely more in good works than in sound opinions." (CS p. 123) |
| "Hearing God's voice in scripture isn't simply a matter of precise, technical expertise. It's a matter of love." (SC p. 189) | "We must come within the **understanding distance**. There is a distance which is properly called the speaking distance, or the hearing distance; beyond which the voice reaches not, and the ear hears not… as it respects God, there is an understanding distance. All beyond that distance cannot understand God; all within it, can easily understand him in all matters of piety and morality. **God himself is the centre of that circle, and humility is its circumference.**… while the philological principles and rules of interpretation enable many men to be skilful in biblical criticism… who neither perceive nor admire the things represented by those words; the sound eye contemplates the things themselves, and is ravished with the moral scenes which the Bible unfolds." (CS, "Rules of Interpretation," Rule 7, pp. 17–18) |

*Honest difference:* Campbell's "means of grace" are Lockean. The word works by bringing "moral facts" before the mind, not by a sacramental meeting of heaven and earth. Wright's view is richer. Still, both deny that the Bible is a rulebook to be parsed: for Campbell it is the channel through which God draws his image on the heart.
