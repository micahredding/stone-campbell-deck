# N. T. Wright as Restorationist

Read with Stone-Campbell ears, N. T. Wright makes the founders' case from the New Testament itself. The church is a taste of new creation, and its substance is unity. That unity must be visible: against denominations, against division, against a drab uniformity, and for freedom of conscience. It is practiced at the table, in baptism, in singing and in scripture. It is the church's testimony to the powers, and it draws the world.

Wright's language only. The sources are *God's Homecoming* (2025), *The Vision of Ephesians* (2025) and *Simply Christian* (2006); see "About the citations" at the end.

<summary>1. The church as a taste of new creation</summary>


> "The way I have come to see this in recent years is to understand the calling of the church as being central to the purposes of God for creation and new creation, and thus to think of the vocation of the church as the call to live as **the small working model of new creation**. This theme is found right across the New Testament, but Ephesians has a claim to be its clearest exposition."

— VE §1

> "The church already in the present time is to be the heaven-plus-earth community, the earthly community suffused with the reality of heaven… And we are justified, set right with God, in order to be that community, and to be it visibly and shockingly before the watching world."

— VE §1

> "We live in the present as the new creation in miniature, the advance working model of what God intends to do in the end."

— VE §5 (Eph 3:20–21)

> "The present filling of the church with God's own powerful life is the sign of what God is going to do for the whole creation. The very existence of the church is thus, in Paul's mind, even if it's hard for us to comprehend it, the sign of hope for the world."

— VE §5

> "The church is to be the advance sign to the world of the new creation, the new heaven-and-earth reality, which God plans to make."

— VE §2 (Eph 1:3–14)

> "The life of the church is supposed to be a forward pointer to the long-promised cosmic reality."

— GH p. 220

> "…because in the future life we will be sinless, and united across all ethnic and other differences, it is all the more important that we anticipate this as far as possible in the present. God's homecoming in Messiah and spirit has already become a reality. It is not something for which we are still waiting."

— GH p. 273


</details>

<details class="sec" markdown="1">
<summary>2. Its substance is unity</summary>


> "Ephesians is all about the church, and especially about its unity. God the Creator always intended to bring together in the Messiah all things in heaven and on earth (Ephesians 1:10), and the coming together of Judaean and Gentile in the Messiah's family is the remarkable and powerful sign of that in the present."

— GH p. 260

> "The vision for a multiethnic family of God was not an add-on to the primary message of one's sins being forgiven so one could go to heaven; the multiethnic family of God, accomplished through the sin-forgiving work of Jesus and the renewal of the spirit, simply was and is the primary message."

— GH p. 264

> "That unifying across traditional boundaries is the sure sign, every bit as sure as Jesus's resurrection, that sin and idolatry has indeed been dealt with on the cross."

— GH p. 260

> "…dogma and ethics really are there. But they are there within that vision of the church as the small working model of new creation, with the coming together of heaven and earth symbolised by the coming together of Judaean and Gentile."

— VE §1

> Eph 4:4–6 reads like "a kind of 'seven spiritual laws': one body, one spirit, one hope; one lord, one faith, one baptism; and, above all, one God… the one-ness runs through all we are and do, making us stand out in a radically and multiply divided world." "Paul could no more imagine there being different 'body of Messiah' communities than he could imagine different spirits, or a different hope."

— VE §6 (Eph 4:4–6)

> "In the multicultural, multiethnic church, joined together as a single family, we glimpse the truth of the gospel, the hope of all creation being liberated from corruption, the hope of the whole world being filled with God's glory as the waters cover the sea."

— GH p. 272


</details>

<details class="sec" markdown="1">
<summary>3. Unity must be visible, in actions and practices</summary>


<details class="sub" markdown="1">
<summary>3a. Against denominations</summary>


> "That last phrase is often translated, or at least cashed out, as 'spiritual unity'. Unfortunately, some have taken that phrase as a way of saying that we have a 'spiritual' unity and therefore don't need to worry about visible and organisational unity. But this term cannot serve as an excuse for disunity on the ground. **Paul would be shocked by the very idea of 'denominations', sitting close to one another on the same street but not even trying to join up to share in mission and resources.**"

— VE §6 (Eph 4:3)

> "…we have taken our eyes off the ball of the church's true vocation through the disastrous habit of developing churches and 'denominations' which reflect different ethnicities or social classes and groups. And, along with that bad habit, we have developed selective readings of scripture which conveniently dodge round passages like the present one."

— VE §5 (Eph 3:10)

> "Some might think: As long as folk are being trained in heavenly-mindedness, why should it matter if people who look and sound different from ourselves (and who perhaps 'do things a bit differently') are worshipping in a different building, half a mile down the road, rather than 'with one heart and voice' together with ourselves? But if we hold to the biblical hope… then it matters enormously."

— GH p. 273


</details>

<details class="sub" markdown="1">
<summary>3b. Against division</summary>


> "The whole picture comes together to insist that it makes no sense to think or act in terms of factions or divisions. We belong to one another and must constantly be on guard against fragmentation. Or – to put it another way – **we must constantly be looking for ways to make our God-given unity a reality in visible practice and mutual love.**"

— VE §6 (Eph 4:4–6)

> "We can't read Ephesians 4 and then imagine that our present multiple disunities don't really matter."

— VE §6

> "Paul as a pastor knew only too well how easily Jesus' followers can trip one another up with misunderstandings, personality clashes and cultural rivalry. That must all go, he says. Live up to your calling! Generous grace was how you began; generous grace in your common life is how you are to continue."

— VE §6 (Eph 4:1–3)

> "Unity, after all, is quite easy if you don't care about holiness. You just get together and turn a blind eye to heresy and sin. Likewise, holiness is quite easy if you don't care about unity: whenever you have a disagreement, you split off and do your own thing. But Paul is emphatic that both matter."

— VE §1

> "The huge multicultural gathering of Revelation 7 is not meant as a vision simply for the ultimate future. It is to be reflected in actual worshipping communities here and now."

— GH p. 273

> "Jesus' followers are to display God's gracious new creation to the world. They're not just to talk about it. They're to display it."

— VE §4 (Eph 2:7)


</details>

<details class="sub" markdown="1">
<summary>3c. Against a drab uniformity, and for freedom of conscience</summary>


> "The community of which Paul speaks is precisely polychrome… **The principalities and powers always tend toward a monochrome, drab uniformity, squashing everything into the same shape and dealing violently with the bits that refuse to conform.** But God's creation isn't like that, and his new creation isn't supposed to be like that either."

— GH p. 276

> "Here's the trick: unity doesn't mean uniformity, but pluriformity doesn't mean chaos."

— VE §6 (Eph 4:11–13)

> "Let's be clear: equality does not mean uniformity."

— GH p. 277

> "Unity does not mean identity, and differentiation does not mean oppressive hierarchy."

— VE §8 (Eph 5:21–6:9)

> "That is why the mutual 'welcome,' as Paul begins it in Romans chapter 14, is so vital: 'Welcome someone who is weak in faith, but not in order to have disputes on difficult points'… Paul is careful to lay out his foundational argument for mutual welcome, in chapter 14, without being specific about the ethnic connotations ('some of us like doing things this way, some prefer to do them that way, so don't judge one another—leave that to the Messiah!'). This is not, we should note, a first-century version of a laissez-faire Enlightenment doctrine of 'tolerance.'… The western Enlightenment has tried to achieve by tolerance what in scripture is God's gift of love."

— GH p. 270

> On differences over worship: "The rest is footnotes, temperament, tradition, and—let's face it—individual likes and dislikes (which is what I call them when they're mine) and irrational prejudices (which is what I call them when they're yours). And at that point the two great commands in the Law (loving God, loving our neighbor) ought to remind us what to do. As Christians we should expect to have demands made on our charity and our patience."

— SC p. 157

*Note: Wright's "polychrome" is mainly ethnic and cultural diversity. Applying it to freedom of conscience is our step; his own bridge is the Romans 14 welcome above.*


</details>


</details>

<details class="sec" markdown="1">
<summary>4. Unity practiced: Communion, Baptism, Singing, Scripture</summary>


<details class="sub" markdown="1">
<summary>4a. Communion</summary>


> "The Eucharist, as many theologians have seen, defines the church: 'There is one loaf; well, then, there may be several of us, but we are one body, because we all share the one loaf.'"

— GH p. 254 (1 Cor 10:17)

> "The bread and the wine can be seen as true gifts coming forward to us from God's future, like the grapes from the promised land that the Israelites were able to sample while still in the wilderness… We are presented, close up and personally, with new creation that we can eat and drink. The church is thus refreshed, again and again, as the small working model of new creation."

— GH p. 252

> "The Eucharist is to the material world what the Sabbath is to the prominent Judaean view of time: it is the advance reality of what we are promised in the end."

— GH p. 253

> "…in the bread-breaking we are tasting God's new creation—the new creation whose prototype and origin is Jesus himself."

— SC p. 154

> "Paul says that 'as often as you eat the bread and drink the cup, you proclaim the Lord's death until he comes' (1 Corinthians 11:26). He doesn't mean that it's a good opportunity for a sermon. Like a handshake or a kiss, doing it says it."

— SC p. 154

> "…when Jesus wanted to explain to his followers what his forthcoming death would mean, and how they could be folded within that meaning, he didn't give them a theory; he gave them a meal."

— GH p. 243

> "When the risen Jesus met two disciples on the road to Emmaus, their hearts burned within them while he talked about the Bible. But their eyes were opened, and they recognized him, when he broke the bread."

— SC p. 153

> "Let's not keep robbing ourselves and our churches of the full enjoyment of the central act of Christian worship by making this meal an occasion of strife."

— SC p. 157

> "Once we have tasted new creation, we are commissioned to be new-creation people."

— GH p. 254


</details>

<details class="sub" markdown="1">
<summary>4b. Baptism</summary>


> "Baptism was thus from the beginning about the double homecoming of God, the arrival of Israel's God and the promise of the spirit. It was issuing a summons, calling people to become part of God's renewed people."

— GH p. 236

> "The picture of the early church, engaging with rulers and authorities in Judaea and in the wider Greco-Roman world, is a picture of a new kind of community, cutting across the normal dividing lines in society, and living—to the consternation of onlookers and officials alike—as a new version of the human race, a small working model of new creation. Baptism, in other words, immerses the followers of Jesus into the Jesus-shaped life."

— GH p. 238

> On "one baptism" (Eph 4:5): "Here Paul insists that baptism is the same across the board."

— VE §6


</details>

<details class="sub" markdown="1">
<summary>4c. Singing</summary>


> "In verse 18, after the command not to get drunk with wine, the next verb is the imperative 'Be filled with the spirit', which then governs all the participles that follow… the fact of being filled with the spirit is expressed in, and emerges through, the mutual encouragement in singing and making heartfelt melody… **for Paul, what 'being filled with the spirit' looks like (anticipating, we recall, God's filling of all creation) is a life of music and thanksgiving, of delight that turns into song.**"

— VE §7 (Eph 5:18–20)

> "All this flows directly from 4:1–6: one body, one spirit, one hope, one lord, one faith, one baptism, one God. As the community is formed in praise, in the same way, that praise, cementing our unity, replaces in our wounded or worried hearts the frustrations and tensions that so easily turn into evil speech or evil deeds."

— VE §7

> "One mind and one mouth; that is Paul's intention." (on Rom 15:5–6)

— GH p. 272

> "The ground is even at the foot of the cross. But those who stand on that even ground—and together sing their hearts out in joy and gratitude—are, and remain, a community drawn from every nation, people, tribe, and tongue."

— GH p. 277


</details>

<details class="sub" markdown="1">
<summary>4d. Scripture</summary>


> "Supposing scripture, like the sacraments, is one of the points where heaven and earth overlap and interlock? Like all other such places, this is mysterious."

— SC p. 181

> "Reading scripture, like praying and sharing in the sacraments, is one of the means by which the life of heaven and the life of earth interlock. (This is what older writers were referring to when they spoke of 'the means of grace.' It isn't that we can control God's grace, but that there are, so to speak, places to go where God has promised to meet with his people…)"

— SC p. 188

> "The Bible, in fact, is not simply an authoritative description of a saving plan, as though it were just an aerial photograph of a particular piece of landscape. It is part of the saving plan itself."

— SC pp. 185–186

> "Hearing God's voice in scripture isn't simply a matter of precise, technical expertise. It's a matter of love."

— SC p. 189

> "Reading the Bible aloud is always central to Christian worship… Reading scripture in worship is, first and foremost, the central way of celebrating who God is and what he's done."

— SC p. 150

> "The new converts would indeed have been powerfully aware of the presence of Jesus in word, in sacrament and in the koinonia of the church."

— VE §3 (Eph 1:15–23)


</details>


</details>

<details class="sec" markdown="1">
<summary>5. Christian unity as a testimony to the powers</summary>


> "In other words, the church is designed, intended—and equipped by the spirit!—to display the multicolored reality of God's original creation, as **the signpost to the principalities and powers that think they run the world**. A new creation, a new way of being human, has arrived in their midst."

— GH p. 265 (Eph 3:9–10)

> "The point is this: when the church is really being the church, the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places are forced to take notice."

— VE §5 (Eph 3:10)

> "As we shall see in Ephesians 3, it is when the church displays its true cross-cultural unity that the powers of the world are confronted with a new reality which they could never have produced themselves."

— VE §1

> "The Greek word which I've translated 'in all its rich variety' is the adjective *polypoikilos*, a word that one might use for 'all the colours of the rainbow'… The new creation is to be every bit as full of colour, life and variety as the original creation, only much more so. And the church is to be the small working model of that new creation."

— VE §5

> "None of this should distract us from the clarion call to the church to be the kind of community which makes the principalities and powers realise that Jesus is lord and they are not."

— VE §5

> "…the church's responsibility, not only to speak the truth to power, but also to live the truth in the face of power."

— VE §5

> "…the higher your Christology and ecclesiology, the more you will find yourself in the thick of the battle, as God's long-awaited new creation comes up against the destructive powers of darkness."

— VE §1

> "They must see that fact as the sign that their very existence has rattled the cages of the powers that think they rule the world. They don't. The church, in all its rich variety (social variety, ethnic variety and so on) is displaying the creator's new-creational wisdom to the world."

— VE §5

> On the Lord's Supper: "If the meal itself proclaims Jesus's victory over evil, it was always likely (as Paul warned in Ephesians 6) that the principalities and powers would try to deflect this challenge to their rule… That will ensure that, when people think of the bread-breaking, they will think, not of Jesus and his kingdom-bringing death and resurrection, but of 'them and us,' and of 'how they do it' as against 'how we do it.'"

— GH p. 244


</details>

<details class="sec" markdown="1">
<summary>6. Christian unity will draw the world</summary>


> "The church is meant to be neither more nor less than the community that displays God's homecoming to the world. That community life has always startled outsiders, provoking some to fierce opposition but **convincing many (including former persecutors) of the truth of the gospel**."

— GH p. 220

> "The church, united and holy, is designed to demonstrate to the hostile watching world that the God revealed in Jesus is the true God, the creator, redeemer, lover and glory of the whole world."

— VE §1

> "It looks like a family such as nobody in Ephesus had ever imagined, let alone seen. The aim is to get people to scratch their heads and think, 'You mean people can actually live like that?' – and then to ask why, and to want to know how they can be part of this new way of life."

— VE §7

> "The point is, rather, as Paul says, so that the world may now see what the heaven-and-earth combination looks like… It's almost as though Paul is expounding what Jesus said in the Sermon on the Mount: 'you must shine your light in front of people', so that 'they will see what wonderful things you do, and they'll give glory to your father in heaven'."

— VE §4 (Eph 2:7)

> "So the message comes loudest and clearest from a community that is embodying the same love in reaching out to their neighbors in every way possible."

— GH p. 230

> "…the very existence of this church, worshipping together as a single family across traditional and ethnic lines, is the sign, both to believers and to the wider world, of the Isaianic hope, the Romans 8 hope, the hope for the new creation in which all things are put right."

— GH p. 270

> "…the church becomes, please God, a family of people who call forth praise from the wider world, people who cause the world to give God glory."

— VE §7 (on the church's singing and thanksgiving)

> "…the God who has come home in Jesus, who comes home in the presence and power of the spirit, and who will come again to complete the work of cosmic renewal toward which the church is called to act as a signpost, a beacon of hope."

— GH p. 274
