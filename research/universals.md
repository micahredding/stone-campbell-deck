# The universals: priesthood, governance, baptism, the table

Research for the class's larger argument: the Church of Christ stands for what is most universal in the
church (the priesthood of all, the governance of all, a succession conferred in baptism, baptism and the
table as uniting practices), and is distinctive where others have let one of these slip.

Every quotation below was checked against the source linked with it (October 2026). "Verified" means the
wording was read in that source; anything not yet checked is marked **to verify**.

## The early church
- **Cyprian of Carthage, c. 250, Epistle 5.4** (ANF trans.): "I made up my mind to do nothing on my own private opinion, without your advice and without the consent of the people." Verified: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050605.htm
- **Leo the Great, Letter 10.6** (to the bishops of Vienne, 444; NPNF trans.): "He who is to govern all, should be chosen by all." and "The consent of the clergy, the testimony of those held in honour, the approval of the orders and the laity should be required." Verified: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3604010.htm (Latin, often cited as *qui praefuturus est omnibus, ab omnibus eligatur*: **to verify** in PL 54.)

## Luther
- **That a Christian Assembly or Congregation Has the Right and Power to Judge All Teaching and to Call, Appoint, and Dismiss Teachers** (1523; WA 11:406ff.; trans. A. T. W. Steinhaeuser, Philadelphia ed. vol. 4, 1931, copyright status **to verify** before publishing the whole). Verified wording: https://godrules.net/library/luther/NEW1luther_d6.htm
  - "the sheep are to judge whether what they teach is the voice of Christ or the voice of strangers."
  - "All teachers should and must, therefore, be subject with their teaching to the judgment of the hearers."
  - "Among Christians everyone is the others judge and, on the other hand, also subject to the other."
  - "no one can deny that every Christian has Gods Word and is taught of God and anointed by Him to the priesthood."
  - On 1 Cor 14:30–31: "Take this passage as a most sure basis, which gives more than sufficient authority to the Christian congregation to preach, to permit men to preach, and to call preachers."
  - "If the bishop does not confirm him, he is none the less confirmed by virtue of the call of the congregation."
  - Translator's introduction: "a clear expression of Luthers ideal of congregationalism, a position which he never gave up, though later he was compelled under the stress of circumstances to content himself with state rule."

## Orthodox
- **Encyclical of the Eastern Patriarchs, 1848, §17:** "neither Patriarchs nor Councils could then have introduced novelties amongst us, because the protector of religion is the very body of the Church, even the people themselves". Verified: https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/mod/1848orthodoxencyclical.asp
- ***Axios!*** ("He is worthy!"), shouted three times by the people at every ordination; historically the people's consent from the time clergy were elected by the whole community (OrthodoxWiki: https://orthodoxwiki.org/Axios). Now an acclamation, not a vote.
- **Alexander Schmemann, *For the Life of the World* (1963):**
  - "The first, the basic definition of man is that he is the priest." (p. 15, as cited)
  - "Religion is needed where there is a wall of separation between God and man. But Christ Who is both God and man has broken down the wall between man and God. He has inaugurated a new life, not a new religion." (quoted in https://www.oca.org/News/headline-news/holy-week-at-st.-tikhons-monastery-confecting-chrism; page **to verify** in the book)
- **Nicolas Afanasiev, *The Church of the Holy Spirit*** (trans. Vitaly Permiakov, Notre Dame, 2012), as quoted in Peter C. Phan's review (https://catholicbooksreview.org/2013/afanasiev.html):
  - "The gift of the Spirit that every member of the faithful receives in the sacrament of initiation is the charism of royal priesthood" (p. 3). His chapter 2 is titled "The Ordination of Laics".
  - "There were only concrete churches in early Christianity, located in a specific place… the Church resided, lived, and was revealed in all the fullness of its unity and in all the unity of its fullness in each local church" (p. 255).
  - "Juridical power is a substitute for love in actual social life, a substitute as perfect as possible in a very imperfect life. In the Church, where perfect love dwells, there is no need for such a substitute" (p. 274). For the "beauty, not legalism" case.
- **John Zizioulas:** treats the laity as an ordained order (secondary sources only; exact wording **to verify** in *Being as Communion*).

## Catholic
- **Vatican II, *Sacrosanctum Concilium* §14:** "Such participation by the Christian people as 'a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a redeemed people' (1 Pet. 2:9; cf. 2:4-5), is their right and duty by reason of their baptism." §30: "the people should be encouraged to take part by means of acclamations, responses, psalmody, antiphons, and songs". Verified: vatican.va
- **Vatican II, *Lumen Gentium*:** §10 Christ "made the new people 'a kingdom and priests to God the Father'"; §12 "The entire body of the faithful, anointed as they are by the Holy One, cannot err in matters of belief"; §32 "all share a true equality with regard to the dignity and to the activity common to all the faithful"; §37 the laity are "permitted and sometimes even obliged to express their opinion on those things which concern the good of the Church". Verified: vatican.va
- **International Theological Commission, *Sensus Fidei in the Life of the Church* (2014):** §1 "all of the baptised participate in the prophetic office of Jesus Christ"; §2 "the faithful have an instinct for the truth of the Gospel". Verified: vatican.va
- **Pope Francis, Oct. 17, 2015** (50th anniversary of the Synod of Bishops): "A synodal Church is a Church which listens, which realizes that listening 'is more than simply hearing'. It is a mutual listening in which everyone has something to learn." … "a principle dear to the Church of the first millennium: 'Quod omnes tangit ab omnibus tractari debet'." … "the flock likewise has an instinctive ability to discern the new ways that the Lord is revealing to the Church." … "in this Church, as in an inverted pyramid, the top is located beneath the base. Consequently, those who exercise authority are called 'ministers', because, in the original meaning of the word, they are the least of all." Verified: vatican.va
- **Synod on Synodality, Final Document (Oct. 26, 2024),** received by Francis as ordinary magisterium (English PDF: https://www.synod.va/content/dam/synod/news/2024-10-26_final-document/ENG---Documento-finale.pdf):
  - §21 "Baptism is the foundation of Christian life… There is nothing higher than this baptismal dignity, equally bestowed upon each person".
  - §22 "The anointing by the Holy Spirit received at Baptism (cf. 1 Jn 2:20.27) enables all believers to possess an instinct for the truth of the Gospel."
  - §23 "All Christians participate in the sensus fidei through Baptism. Therefore, as well as constituting the basis of synodality, Baptism is also the foundation of ecumenism."

## The ecumenical consensus: the Lima text
- **World Council of Churches, *Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry* (Faith and Order Paper 111, Lima, 1982),** the most widely studied ecumenical document, with Catholic and Orthodox theologians on the commission (PDF: https://www.oikoumene.org/sites/default/files/Document/FO1982_111_en.pdf):
  - Baptism §6: "Our common baptism, which unites us to Christ in faith, is thus a basic bond of unity."
  - Eucharist §31: "As the eucharist celebrates the resurrection of Christ, it is appropriate that it should take place at least every Sunday."
  - Ministry, commentary on §17: "The New Testament never uses the term 'priesthood' or 'priest' (hiereus) to designate the ordained ministry or the ordained minister. In the New Testament, the term is reserved, on the one hand, for the unique priesthood of Jesus Christ and, on the other hand, for the royal and prophetic priesthood of all baptized."

## The Southern Baptist contrast (*Baptist Faith and Message*, 2000; https://bfm.sbc.net/bfm2000/)
- Keeps congregational governance: a church is "an autonomous local congregation of baptized believers… Each congregation operates under the Lordship of Christ through democratic processes."
- Keeps believer's immersion, but as "an act of obedience symbolizing", and "Being a church ordinance, it is prerequisite to the privileges of church membership and to the Lord's Supper" (a closed table).
- The Supper is "a symbolic act of obedience", memorial, and in practice usually not weekly.
- So what slips is not governance but the centrality of the two practices: the Supper every Lord's day (which even the Lima text asks for), and baptism and the table as uniting rather than fencing.
