The founders on unity, in their own words. Barton W. Stone from the Christian Messenger (archive.org OCR, in the research database 91 Extracted Text/Primary Periodicals/Christian Messenger, OCR slips corrected); Thomas Campbell from the class site’s full text of the Declaration and Address.

Barton W. Stone, “Notice,” Christian Messenger 9 (1835), p. 285

For nine years I have edited the Christian Messenger, and this is the ninth volume. For 32 years of my ministry I have kept in view the unity of Christians as my polar star. For this I have labored, for this have suffered reproach, persecution and privation of ease, the loss of friendship, wealth and honor from men. The Bible I have recommended as the only divine and safe ground upon which the different parties of Christians can meet.

Thomas Campbell, Declaration and Address (1809), the Address

With you all we desire to unite in the bonds of an entire Christian unity—Christ alone being the head, the center, his word the rule; an explicit belief of, and manifest conformity to it, in all things—the terms.

Union in truth has been, and ever must be, the desire and prayer of all such; “Union in Truth” is our motto.
