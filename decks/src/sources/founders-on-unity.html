Barton W. Stone, “An Address to the Churches of Christ,” Christian Messenger 6 (1832)

All believe that immersion is baptism—why should they who submit to the one baptism contend and separate because they do not exactly view every design of it alike? … Let the unity of Christians be our polar star. To this let our eyes be continually turned, and to this let our united efforts be directed—that the world may believe, and be saved.

Barton W. Stone, “Notice,” Christian Messenger 9 (1835), p. 285

For nine years I have edited the Christian Messenger, and this is the ninth volume. For 32 years of my ministry I have kept in view the unity of Christians as my polar star. For this I have labored, for this have suffered reproach, persecution and privation of ease, the loss of friendship, wealth and honor from men. The Bible I have recommended as the only divine and safe ground upon which the different parties of Christians can meet.

Thomas Campbell, Declaration and Address (1809), the Address

Resume that precious, that dear-bought liberty, wherewith Christ has made his people free; a liberty from subjection to any authority but his own, in matters of religion.

With you all we desire to unite in the bonds of an entire Christian unity—Christ alone being the head, the center, his word the rule; an explicit belief of, and manifest conformity to it, in all things—the terms.

Union in truth has been, and ever must be, the desire and prayer of all such; “Union in Truth” is our motto.

Alexander Campbell, reply to a sister in Lunenburg, Virginia, Millennial Harbinger (September 1837), p. 411 · as reprinted in B. L. Smith, The Millennial Harbinger Abridged (1902), vol. 2, pp. 379–380

In reply to this conscientious sister, I observe, that if there be no Christians in the Protestant sects, there are certainly none among the Romanists, none among the Jews, Turks, Pagans; and therefore no Christians in the world except ourselves, or such of us as keep, or strive to keep, all the commandments of Jesus. Therefore, for many centuries there has been no church of Christ, no Christians in the world; and the promises concerning the everlasting kingdom of Messiah have failed, and the gates of hell have prevailed against his church! This cannot be; and therefore there are Christians among the sects.

Alexander Campbell, A Debate between Rev. A. Campbell and Rev. N. L. Rice (Lexington, 1843; printed 1844), p. 559 · archive.org, debatebetweenre00camp

I do sincerely rejoice, that simple, honest mistakes, where they are not the result of corruption of heart, will not, in my opinion, preclude any Pedo-baptist from heaven, although on earth he should, through his mistakes, never enjoy the full reign of heaven in his soul. The Judge of all the earth will do right. I circumscribe not the Divine philanthropy—the Divine grace. I dare not say that there is no salvation in the church of Rome, or in that of Constantinople; though, certainly, Protestants do not regard them as churches builded upon the foundation of apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ being the chief corner-stone. In all the Protestant parties there are many excellent spirits, that mourn over the desolations of Zion—that love the gospel and its author most sincerely.

Alexander Campbell, The Christian System (1839), “Foundation of Christian Union,” p. 115 (archive.org OCR, in the research database; small capitals and OCR slips restored)

But that the reader may have before his mind in one summary view the whole scheme of union and co-operation, which the Living Oracles and the present state of the Christian religion in the world demand; which has been at different times, and in various manners, illustrated and sustained in the present controversy, against divisions,—we shall here submit it in one period. Let the Bible be substituted for all human creeds; facts, for definitions; things, for words; faith, for speculation; unity of faith, for unity of opinion; the positive commandments of God, for human legislation and tradition; piety, for ceremony; morality, for partizan zeal; the practice of religion, for the mere profession of it: and the work is done.
