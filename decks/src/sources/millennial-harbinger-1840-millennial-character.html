Millennial Character of the Harbinger.

We have often rather jeeringly been asked, “Wherein consists the millennial characteristics of the Harbinger?”—the querists imagining that a millennial harbinger must be always discussing or preaching millenniary affairs. When we put to sea under this banner we had the port of Primitive Christianity, in letter and spirit, in profession and practice, in our eye; reasoning that all the Millennium we could scripturally expect was not merely the restoration of the Jerusalem church in all its moral and religious characters, but the extension of it through all nations and languages for one thousand years. To prepare the way for such a development of Christianity several things are essential—

1st. The annihilation of partyism.

2d. The restoration of a pure speech.

3d. The preaching of the original gospel.

4th. The restoration of the Christian ordinances.

5th. Larger measures of the Holy Spirit, as promised to those who seek for it in the appointed way.

To these five points, as means of the triumph of the gospel over idolatry, infidelity, impiety, immorality, and corrupt religion, we have for years been directing the inquiries of our readers. But we have not committed ourselves to any of the theories of the present day on the nature and coming of the Millennium. We have, indeed, opened our pages to brother M‘Korkle and the Reformed Clergyman, and we have given various extracts and some views of the two systems of interpretation; but we have not gone spiritedly, nor learnedly, nor exclusively into the arena of controversy on this subject—for three reasons—

1st. We wanted the full assurance of understanding the prophecies necessary to our taking a decided stand.

2d. We feared the identification of any theory of the Millennium—as much as any theory of physics or of metaphysics—with the great principles which must leaven society before any great or useful moral change could be expected; judging that if we missed, or were supposed to have missed the mark, either in a millennial theory or in any prominent interpretation of prophecy, it would be used by our vigilant and assiduous opponents against the whole burthen of our lives. Our generous enemies would put down every item of reformation for which we contend, by affirming that it was a part of a theory of prophecy, or of a millennial church, &c. &c. should we have given them any chance by any mistake or error on that subject. On this account I have always doubted the prudence of those brethren who either preached or wrote much on the subject.

3d. We had more pressing and important matter on hand.

But we have not been all this time negligent of the matter, nor inattentive to what is passing in the world on the second coming of the Messiah, the Prophets, and the Millennium. We have read, reflected, and conversed a good deal on these themes, and now think it due to our readers, to the controversy now pending on the question of the second coming of the Messiah, that we should, in our own proper editorial character, deliver some essays on these themes in our next volume. Millenniary affairs, and the general subject of unfulfilled prophecy, will, in our next volume, command a little more of our attention. We may now dive a little into the future without the jeopardy of any principle of moral and religious truth for which we have been laboring for some years.

A. C.
