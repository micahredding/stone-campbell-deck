Excerpts from across the church on the priesthood of all believers, the governance of all, baptism, and the table: the early church, Luther, Orthodox and Catholic teaching, and the ecumenical Lima text. Each excerpt was checked against the source named with it (October 2026); see research/universals.md in the deck repository for links.

Cyprian of Carthage, Epistle 5.4 (c. 250) · Ante-Nicene Fathers translation

…I made up my mind to do nothing on my own private opinion, without your advice and without the consent of the people.

Leo the Great, Letter 10.6, to the bishops of the province of Vienne (444) · Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers translation

He who is to govern all, should be chosen by all. The consent of the clergy, the testimony of those held in honour, the approval of the orders and the laity should be required.

Martin Luther, That a Christian Assembly or Congregation Has the Right and Power to Judge All Teaching and to Call, Appoint, and Dismiss Teachers (1523) · trans. A. T. W. Steinhaeuser (Philadelphia edition, vol. 4)

Bishops, pope, theologians, and anyone else have the power to teach; but the sheep are to judge whether what they teach is the voice of Christ or the voice of strangers.

All teachers should and must, therefore, be subject with their teaching to the judgment of the hearers.

Among Christians everyone is the other’s judge and, on the other hand, also subject to the other.

For no one can deny that every Christian has God’s Word and is taught of God and anointed by Him to the priesthood.

If the bishop does not confirm him, he is none the less confirmed by virtue of the call of the congregation.

Encyclical of the Eastern Patriarchs (1848), §17

Moreover, neither Patriarchs nor Councils could then have introduced novelties amongst us, because the protector of religion is the very body of the Church, even the people themselves, who desire their religious worship to be ever unchanged and of the same kind as that of their fathers…

Alexander Schmemann, For the Life of the World (1963)

The first, the basic definition of man is that he is the priest.

Religion is needed where there is a wall of separation between God and man. But Christ Who is both God and man has broken down the wall between man and God. He has inaugurated a new life, not a new religion.

Nicolas Afanasiev, The Church of the Holy Spirit · trans. Vitaly Permiakov (Notre Dame, 2012)

The gift of the Spirit that every member of the faithful receives in the sacrament of initiation is the charism of royal priesthood. (p. 3)

Juridical power is a substitute for love in actual social life, a substitute as perfect as possible in a very imperfect life. In the Church, where perfect love dwells, there is no need for such a substitute. (p. 274)

Second Vatican Council, Sacrosanctum Concilium (1963), §14

Such participation by the Christian people as “a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a redeemed people” (1 Pet. 2:9; cf. 2:4-5), is their right and duty by reason of their baptism.

Second Vatican Council, Lumen Gentium (1964), §12

The entire body of the faithful, anointed as they are by the Holy One, cannot err in matters of belief.

Pope Francis, address on the 50th anniversary of the Synod of Bishops (October 17, 2015)

But in this Church, as in an inverted pyramid, the top is located beneath the base. Consequently, those who exercise authority are called “ministers”, because, in the original meaning of the word, they are the least of all.

Synod on Synodality, Final Document (October 26, 2024)

§21 Baptism is the foundation of Christian life. … There is nothing higher than this baptismal dignity, equally bestowed upon each person…

§23 All Christians participate in the sensus fidei through Baptism. Therefore, as well as constituting the basis of synodality, Baptism is also the foundation of ecumenism.

World Council of Churches, Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry (the Lima text, 1982)

Baptism §6: Our common baptism, which unites us to Christ in faith, is thus a basic bond of unity.

Eucharist §31: As the eucharist celebrates the resurrection of Christ, it is appropriate that it should take place at least every Sunday.

Ministry, commentary on §17: The New Testament never uses the term “priesthood” or “priest” (hiereus) to designate the ordained ministry or the ordained minister. In the New Testament, the term is reserved, on the one hand, for the unique priesthood of Jesus Christ and, on the other hand, for the royal and prophetic priesthood of all baptized.
