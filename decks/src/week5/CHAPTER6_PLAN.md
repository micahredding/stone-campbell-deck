## The argument (agreed)

1. **No one would ordain them.** Brush Run, a church every court refused (Synod of Pittsburg 1810).
2. **Believers are priests.** 1 Pet 2:9; Rev 1:6. Campbell 1830 (MH p. 368): immersed believers "are all made sons and daughters, kings and priests to God… a right to meet in his name to break the loaf of blessing, to drink the cup of blessing, without the intervention of a mediator, priest, or prophet, consecrated by human hands." Luther 1520: "we are all consecrated as priests by baptism."
3. **What the tradition says, and where it stops** ("what the tradition wants to say and cannot fully voice"). Pairs, with "in necessity" as the refrain:
   - Tertullian: "Are not even we laics priests?" / laymen baptize "in cases of necessity".
   - Luther: all equally priests / "except with the consent of the community, or at the call of a superior"; the desert laymen choose one of themselves.
   - Knox, First Book of Discipline 1560: "It apperteneth to the Pepill… to Elect thair Minister"; laying on of hands "nott necessarie" / lay Supper "companioun to companioun" deserves death. (The Seceders were Knox's heirs.)
   - Calvin IV.3.15: "on the consent and approbation of the people"; IV.19.25 clergy arrogating "what is given to the whole Church" is sacrilege / sacraments belong to ministers (IV.3.16; IV.15.20–22).
   - Cambridge Platform 1648 ch. IX: "where there are no Elders, Imposition of hands may be performed by some of the Brethren" (closest precedent to Brush Run).
   - Catholic: CCC 1268 "a share in the common priesthood"; laypeople baptize in necessity and distribute Communion (canons 861 §2, 230 §3, 910 §2) / LG 10 "differ… in essence"; canon 900, 1012.
   - Didache 15 "appoint for yourselves bishops and deacons"; 1 Clement 44 "with the consent of the whole church".
4. **What does a priest do?** (Micah: important, good framing — the hinge.)
   - Serves at the altar → the table. Campbell 1830 (above); "aspiring priesthood" that will not let "a disciple… pray over the loaf or the cup" (MH 1830 p. 166); "May they not, without a human priest to consecrate the way for them, approach the Lord's table…" (CS p. 306); Ballantine in Stone's CM 1835 "any brother, who can give thanks for a crucified Savior"; Lipscomb 1910 "Every disciple is a priest to offer his own sacrifice"; Campbell CS p. 309 "And thus they as priests feast upon his sacrifice."
   - Sets apart → ordination. Jan 1, 1812 certificate ("according to the principles of this church"); Luther 1523 Leisnig; Campbell CS p. 84 "the multitude of the faithful, are the fountain of official power… Servants made by servants are servants of servants"; Milligan 1868 (pp. 343–352): "THE CHURCH SHOULD ELECT ALL HER OWN OFFICERS"; "the Church herself is the heritage (κλήρος, clergy) of God"; "No one should attempt to serve any Church in any capacity who has not been elected by the suffrages of at least a majority of his brethren"; ordained "by the whole congregation"; ordination "simply a solemn setting apart". (His "wild and reckless democracy" is about decorum in conducting elections, not against the congregation's power.) Prefer "set apart" to "make sacred".
5. **The contrast.** Radical: every church's policy said no — Westminster Confession 27.4 "neither of which may be dispensed by any, but by a minister of the Word lawfully ordained" (in `decks/src/week4map/sources/westminster-confession-american.html`); Catholic canons 900/1012; Anglican Art. 23 (to verify). Yet suggestively pointed to by voices across the tradition. Line: **what the church allowed in an emergency, Brush Run did on purpose** / the tradition's exception became our ordinary Sunday.
6. **Landing.** Open communion (Ch. 1) → open hosting → ministers made by the whole church. Every Sunday at Otter Creek, the table embodies it (quiz callback: "this do in remembrance of me"). Ordination itself is occasional; the weekly act is the table. Say "ordained by the congregation", not that any individual ordains.

## Emphasis rules
- Use Stone-Campbell quotations that AFFIRM the priesthood and tie it to the table, ordination or governance. Do NOT use deflating ones: Campbell "not as the representative of the Lord, but after his example"; Lipscomb "This is not officiating, for any disciple can do this". Stone's recorded positions (only ordained preachers administer; "the church may elect, but not ordain") run against this thread; leave him out of it.
- Women (Micah, 2026-11-02): the movement voiced the ideal ("sons and daughters, kings and priests"; "any disciple") and was inconsistent in not extending it to women. Frame as ideal voiced, not yet extended — parallel to the tradition's "all are priests… in necessity". Put "sons and daughters" on screen.
- Brownlow (1945): affirms "every child of God is a priest" (spiritual sacrifices) and an open table to self-examination, but never ties priesthood to serving/ordaining. Minor.

## Narrative frame: "Stranded" (2026-11-02, in progress with Micah)

Micah: don't lean on Otter Creek's practice in the narrative (it reads as "how we're unique"); that connection comes later. The emergency argument carries it: **an emergency can't create an authority; it can only reveal one that was there all along.** Dramatize it as scenes of believers stranded from any priest, each reaching further, with a flinch (as Luther → Zwingli → Anabaptists dramatized conscience).

1. **The deathbed.** Florence 1439 (Decree for the Armenians): "in case of necessity not only a priest or a deacon, but even a lay man or a woman, even a pagan and a heretic, can baptize." Tertullian, *On Baptism* 17 (laymen, "cases of necessity"; not women); *Exhortation to Chastity* 7: "Are not even we laics priests? … where there is no joint session of the ecclesiastical Order, you offer, and baptize, and are priest, alone for yourself. But where three are, a church is, albeit they be laics." (Montanist-era; an argument about remarriage.)
2. **The desert.** Luther 1520 (*To the Christian Nobility*): laymen "set down in a wilderness… such a man would be as truly a priest as though all bishops and popes had consecrated him." Same treatise, the thesis in his own words: "in cases of necessity every man can baptise and absolve, which would not be possible if we were not all priests."
3. **Leisnig, 1523** (Steinhaeuser tr., *Works of Martin Luther* 4, pp. 80–83; verified in the archive.org scan): "how much more does an entire Christian congregation have the right to call a man to this office whenever it becomes necessary! **And it is always necessary, and never more than now.**" / "necessity is necessity and knows no bounds, just as, when fire breaks out in a town, everyone should hasten to lend a hand" / "since in our days the necessity exists, and there is never a bishop to provide evangelical preachers…" / "If the bishop does not confirm him, he is none the less confirmed by virtue of the call of the congregation." (Leisnig had a patron and a bishop; they would not supply evangelical preachers. Say "no bishop who would give them a preacher", not "no bishop".)
4. **The flinch.** Calvin *Inst.* IV.15.20–22 shuts even emergency lay baptism ("cannot … be defended"; "in baptism by women, the sin is the greater"). *First Book of Discipline* 1560 (Knox and five others): the people elect, hands "nott necessarie", yet lay Suppers "companioun to companioun" "deserve death" (Laing 2:189, 253–254).
5. **Across the ocean.** Cambridge Platform 1648 IX: "where there are no Elders, Imposition of hands may be performed by some of the Brethren… (occasion & need so requiring)."
6. **Brush Run.** Refused by the Synod of Pittsburg (1810), with no court to turn to (the Baptists came in 1813, so not "stranded for good"). Jan 1, 1812: "according to the principles of this church." Brush Run did the *ordination*; "any brother" at the table is documented only from c. 1827–30 (`Members at the Table`), so don't say Brush Run did both.
7. **The reveal:** what does a priest do? (serves at the table; sets apart). Campbell MH 1830 p. 368, "sons and daughters, kings and priests".
8. **The horizon (the millennial thread; database branch `claude/great-meitner-rklluw`, `The Scottish Independents/McLean's Millennium.md`).** The emergency shows what was true from the beginning; the precursors expected it to be true for everyone at the end. Rev 20:6 "they shall be priests of God and of Christ." McLean, *The Commission* (c. 1797), *Works* I p. 338: "the first resurrection imports, a revival of true and primitive Christianity in the world"; its subjects "consecrated as priests of God and of Christ"; p. 351: "The saints are at present made kings and priests unto God—a kingly priesthood; but then they shall be more eminently so… Then shall that promise be fully accomplished, 'Ye shall be named the priests of the Lord; men shall call you the ministers of our God'" (Isa 61:6; re-checked 2026-11-02). Ewing 1799: neighbours teaching one another, "so successful, as, at last to be unnecessary." Campbell at Brush Run's first Supper: "an emblem of the millennial state of the Church" (already on the Ch. 6 spine); 1830 "the kingdom of the clergy, or antichristian kingdom" (MH 1830 p. 56; per the other session, not re-checked). McLean's own flinch: he kept the Supper for appointed officers (*Works* III p. 435). Bridge to Chapter 7. Card idea: "Not only remembered. *Expected.*"
