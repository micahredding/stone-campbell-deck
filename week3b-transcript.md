## Recap: every instrument of unity failed

**Slides 1–2 · Title · Declaration and Address, part two**

All right so last week we talked about Thomas Campbell's Declaration and Address and I tried to set this in kind of a historical context to give some context for the argument I think Campbell is making

**Slide 3 · The egg (one click per division, then the break)**

and so we talked about the history of the pursuit of unity in the church and how throughout history the church has been seeking through all kinds of instruments of unity, creeds and confessions and magistrates and bishops and Popes and so on to create unity and yet virtually every one of these efforts has failed and even more has often created the very division it was seeking to overcome

**Slide 4 · Why did every instrument fail?**

so we asked the question why why do these instruments of unity fail? Why have they always failed

## Because conscience cannot be compelled

**Slides 5–6 · Worms, 1521 · "Here I stand"**

and the answer I proposed is that it ultimately traces to the fact that we cannot compel conscience and so every time a pope or an emperor or a council or a creed stands and says here is where you must go here is where you must come to reach consensus with the established church there's always someone who can stand in the position of Martin Luther and say I cannot unless I'm convinced by reason and scripture because it is neither safe nor right to go against conscience so here I stand, I can do no other

**Slide 7 · Acts 5:29, "We ought to obey God rather than men"**

and this is not some incidental fact it's actually a deep fact about Christianity itself. It is essentially the fact that we have instilled the virtue of the idea that we must obey God rather than man

**Slide 8 · Every instrument of unity meets someone who says: *I cannot.***

and so this it has been an inescapable feature of Christian history every time we try to create consensus by committee or council or any other thing, we have an individual or a group who will stand up and say I cannot I cannot do it and so these instruments of unity fail and they fail over and over again,

## A long Christian tradition

**Slide 9 · Luther did not invent this: Aquinas, "Even a mistaken conscience binds"**

and we looked at the fact that this idea that you cannot compel conscience you must obey conscience it's it's a deep Christian idea and it's recognized theologically and so Thomas Aquinas centuries before Martin Luther can argue that if the pope, the church, the councils, whoever it may be command you to do something and yet your own conscience tells you otherwise that even if you are excommunicated you're obligated to follow your conscience because your conscience is what you understand as the the word of God, the command of God when you look I understand what God actually wants then your conscience compels you to follow that understanding and you must do that and take the consequences.

> *Note: the excommunication line is from Aquinas's commentary on the* Sentences *(IV d.38), and its meaning is disputed; the slide keeps to the* Summa *(even a mistaken conscience binds). Say it as "Aquinas is often read as saying…".*

**Slide 10 · Catechism §1790 / Vatican II**

This is your ethical duty and and so Martin Luther is just leaning into a long Christian tradition about the primacy of conscience not only over individual actions, but over commands of church council and pope itself

## Claimed, but not extended

**Slides 11–14 · Rome rejects Luther · Luther rejects Zwingli · Zwingli rejects the Anabaptists · Thomas Campbell: How dare you put asunder?**

and at the same time the irony is that that through Christian history this fact can be proclaimed and yet the corresponding grace cannot be extended, and so the Catholic Church can claim this kind of conscience for themselves, but they cannot extend grace over that to Luther and Luther can claim the primacy of conscience for himself, but he cannot extend grace to Zwingli and Zwingli can claim primacy of conscience for himself, but he cannot extend grace to the Anabaptists

**Slide 15 · Luther claimed conscience for himself. Campbell claimed it for his brother.**

so this I think is what makes Thomas Campbell's Declaration and Address such a profound document in the history of American Christianity because Luther can claim freedom of conscience for himself, but Campbell wants to claim it for his brother

## Conscience is how Christ builds the body

**Slide 16 · Freedom of conscience is how Christ has put his body together.**

put differently, Campbell has recognized that the freedom of conscience the inability to compel conscience is in fact a constituent element of the body of Christ. It is an organizing principle of the church, it is how Christ himself has put together the body

**Slides 17–18 · "…no man can judge for his brother" · "…a daring usurpation of his throne"**

and so every effort to thwart that is actually a usurpation of the throne of Christ trying to undo something that Christ has done, and so Campbell can say this in quite strong terms, a daring usurpation of his throne

**Slides 19–20 · Luther: priest, bishop and pope · "Conscience is the aboriginal Vicar of Christ"**

we also saw last week that this understanding of what conscience is is actually present in the Christian tradition at large as well that that Martin Luther can say that every baptized believer is consecrated as a priest, a bishop and a pope, and even the Catholic catechism can tell us that the conscience is the aboriginal Vicar of Christ. The pope is the Vicar of Christ upon earth for the Catholic Church and yet there is a Vicar of Christ who has primacy and that is conscience

**Slides 21–23 · Conscience is an exercise of the priesthood of all believers · So conscience answers to Christ · Not to the pope / council / me / To Christ**

and so the exercise of conscience and the obedience to conscience is not a mere ethical matter or incidental matter certainly it's actually part of the priesthood of all believers when you are baptized into Christ and joined with the body of Christ, you become one with Christ and are filled with his spirit then you have an ethical and deeper obligation to exercise the gift, the calling of priesthood to answer directly to God, and to strive to understand, and put into practice the will of God

## Then how can we be one?

**Slides 24–25 · Christ makes us different · How can Christians have unity, if Christ… makes us so different?**

and so this is the profound position that Campbell stakes out that he actually wants to take this seriously and rather than to simply treat the conscience as sort of a roadblock on the way to Christian unity he wants to treat it as just an inescapable aspect of what it is to be part of the body of Christ to be filled with the spirit of Christ to exercise the priesthood of our baptism then how do Christians how does the church actually attain unity in this case if this is the fact if we are inevitably diverse, and we cannot compel conscience to overcome this. How can we be united

**Slides 26–29 · We will not be united by another list · We will understand differently · Campbell expected it · And would not want it**

well it can't it won't come from any kind of simple uniformity by fiat, so Campbell will recognize this right that the opinions and so forth of the body of Christ are not going to be uniform in this era and we should we should not expect them to be nor even could we anticipate that were they to be uniform, that that would actually be a good thing so it's not our opinions upon which we unite it's not our ability to create an instrument of compelling conscience upon which we unite all of these are impossibilities

## Only Christ

**Slides 30–31 · There can only be one answer. / Christ alone · "…anywhere but in Christ and his simple word"**

so what how is unity to be achieved and of course, the answer the only answer that is possible is Christ himself if Christ is the reason for our diversity and for our our inescapable role of conscience, then Christ is the only one who can actually unite us,

**Slides 32–34 · Every time we hand… a job only Christ can do, it fails · There is nothing wrong with a confession · Proposition 7**

and so the problem is not with confessions and creeds as tools of education Campbell will say this as well that in fact for the purposes of theological education confessions are are good and and the more detailed the better sometimes we look at the Apostles' Creed or Nicene Creed and celebrate them because they are short and and thus maybe easier to get people to agree on but Campbell's point is no if we're educating people then then we should have as much detail as we have available, but they cannot be the boundary markers of the body of Christ they cannot achieve the unity of the body of Christ and every time we try to use some instrument of unity to do what only Christ himself can do we will only create more division and animosity, and ultimately our purposes will fail

## Christ has already made us one

**Slides 35–36 · Christ already unites us · Proposition 1**

so what do we do with this practically speaking? we know that the church is divided and is always dividing in some sense and the first thing, and the most fundamental thing that Thomas Campbell offers is that Christ in fact has already made us one so this is the first proposition of the Declaration and Address that the church of Christ upon earth is essentially constitutionally one consisting of all those in every place who put their faith in Christ and seek to obey him and of none others

**Slide 37 · Proposition 1, misread: Not only the ones with our name on the sign / Rome / Constantinople / "All those in every place"**

now, sometimes you, if you've heard this quoted in Church of Christ context, you might've heard this used to mean the opposite of what he's saying you might've heard it used to suggest that only people who have the name Church of Christ out front are actually part of the true church the true body of Christ you find some attitudes among Catholic and Orthodox thinkers who will say something to this effect that you know this is the true church and you know, if you are not part of us then you are not part of the true church Orthodox will say this Catholic some Catholics will say that some Orthodox will say this and of course you know some Church of Christ people will say this but that is not what Thomas Campbell is saying it's the opposite of what Thomas Campbell is saying he's saying the Church of Christ upon the earth consists of all those in every place it consists of Christians in all the denominations all the sects all the different divisions they all are already part of the church of Christ and the body of Christ Christ has made them one. We have not made them one and all the attempts that we have made to divide them and to define the boundaries according to our own principles and standards are in fact, erroneous

**Slides 38–40 · David Bentley Hart, "The Myth of Schism" (citation, then two quotes)**

I think David Bentley Hart an Eastern Orthodox theologian he has an essay from years ago, called The Myth of Schism and he's dealing with the 1054 schism between the Catholic Church and the Orthodox Church and he deals with the history of this of the schism and how to think about it, but I think the underlying principle he's getting at is that in fact, the body of Christ the church is made one by Christ and the pope and the bishop and the council, and whoever else has no authority to divide it and further, they have no ability to divide it.

**Slide 41 · No sect ever had the authority, or the ability, to divide what Christ has joined.**

It's constitutionally one because of what Christ himself has done and that's what Thomas Campbell is saying the Church of Christ upon earth is constitutionally one

## Our first duty: recognize it

**Slides 42–43 · Do we recognize it? · 1 Corinthians 11:29, "not discerning the Lord's body"**

so what do we do with this yes, the first thing we do is to recognize it and I think this is part of the reason communion is so significant to this story right in 1 Corinthians 11 Paul critiques the Corinthians because in their practice of the Lord's Supper they are not recognizing the body of Christ. They're not recognizing the body of Christ in their brothers and so he says they're drinking communion they're drinking condemnation upon themselves because they have failed to recognize the body of Christ,

**Slides 44–48 · When Campbell gave the Supper… he was discerning the body · "Holding fast the unity of the spirit" · Proposition 9 · Romans 15:7 · I receive you because Christ received you**

so when Thomas Campbell is extending communion to Christians of a different sect than his this is what he's doing. He's trying to recognize the body of Christ. He's trying to recognize that whatever our sects and sub-sects have done with their divisions and declaring people anathema and so forth they never actually had the authority nor the ability to divide what Christ himself has joined

**Slide 49 · Our first duty: recognize what Christ has already done.**

so the first duty that Campbell is calling us to is to recognize recognize what Christ has already done and to stop pretending that there is something else that has taken precedence over Christ's own declaration and work

## The Word of God, not interpretations of it

**Slides 50–52 · Christ and his word, not our interpretations of his word · Worms, the full sentence · Captive to the Word of God**

now, I wanna come back to something that I brought up last week and this is that Luther and his stance before the Diet of Worms he he declares himself to be captive to the word of God and he says, unless I am convinced by reason and scripture then here I stand I can do no other and what I was trying to suggest is that he's claiming an allegiance to the word of God, not to his opinions on the word of God he's not saying my opinion on this matter at this moment has now become a new creed for me he's saying I have an obligation to the word of God to wrestle with it to grapple with it to come to it and when I come away with an understanding of it then I have an obligation to follow that understanding according to my conscience,

**Slide 53 · Word of God ≠ interpretations of the Word of God**

but that's not an obligation upon you, according to your conscience you're not committed to my understanding or my interpretation you are committed to the word, I am committed to the word of God and if we want to persuade each other about our interpretation then we come together with reason and scripture and try to do so, and so this is I I think critically important for thinking about this concept and this language

**Slides 54–55 · Not trading the Westminster Confession for an agreed reading of the Bible · If we cannot agree on the Westminster Confession, how much less on all of Scripture?**

because in the Church of Christ and in Campbell and Stone, and so on, we will talk about the supremacy of the word of God and what this has often been interpreted as and often been applied as in our circles is a new kind of legalism where we think something like this, Thomas Campbell is throwing out the Westminster Confession and he's he's saying we're all just going to agree on interpreting the Bible and this is not what he's saying not what he can be saying because if it's impossible to agree on interpreting the Westminster Confession then how much more is it impossible to agree on interpretations of the Bible Campbell knows this

**Slides 56–59 · "Christ alone being the head, the center, his word the rule" · Not our understanding / not our interpretation / Christ and Scripture itself · Hold it. Do not require it. · Keep your reading.**

so when he says that he wants us to be united on Christ and his word he's not saying to be united on Christ and a particular interpretation of that word he's saying let's be united on Christ our commitment to Christ our commitment to Christ's word

**Slides 60–61 · Spirit / Scripture / Reason · We are united not by a shared interpretation, but by a shared commitment to Scripture itself.**

and this is not I think an alien concept to most of us every week I meet with other Christians who have different understandings of scripture than I do and we talk about those understandings and we wrestle with them, and sometimes we move closer together in our interpretations, and sometimes we move farther apart in our interpretations but we usually move closer together as members of the body of Christ one way or the other we are not united by a shared commitment to a particular interpretation, but by a shared commitment to scripture itself, and this is and that shared commitment to scripture is part of our shared commitment to Christ again not our interpretation of scripture not our interpretation of Christ. We are united by Christ himself, and we are called into a commitment with the word of God, even when that commitment brings us by its very nature into all diversity of thought

## Landing

**Slide 62 · Recognize the unity Christ has already made, and strive to act accordingly.**

so this is where I think we we land with trying to understand what Campbell is doing and what he's setting out as the principle of unity going forward and simply that we must start by recognizing the existing unity of the body of Christ and striving to act accordingly
