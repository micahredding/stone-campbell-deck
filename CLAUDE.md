- **The class's primary themes (Micah, 2026-11-02).** Frame slides, ideas, side paths and site pages around these:
  1. **Unity:** expressed in an open table, in laying down our own authority (the Last Will and Testament), and in seeking connection with other Christians despite deep differences.
  2. **Freedom of conscience:** connected to Aquinas, Luther, Zwingli and the Anabaptists. Thomas Campbell in the D&A sees it as essential to the nature of the church.
  3. **The priesthood of all believers:** practiced at Brush Run's ordination and in who may serve; often practiced more than proclaimed. Connected with the church's deep tradition that affirms it, including Luther's radical stance (yet limited practice).
  4. **The future (eschatology):** God's future calls us to embody the church's ultimate unity here and now, which leads us to ask how best to practice and testify to it, and ultimately to questions about the table, baptism, congregational singing, preaching, ordination and worship.
  5. Secondarily, **tradition:** the movement restates and deepens elements of the Christian tradition at large, in a radical embrace of some of its deepest ideas and practices.
  On the public site these are the organizing themes (Unity, Freedom, Priesthood, Future, with Tradition as the Great Stream hub).
- **Primary themes, Chapter 5 on:** ordination by the choice of the congregation (so authority lives in the congregation) and serving the table (not only open to all, but hosted by any). Both go straight to the priesthood of all believers; frame slides, ideas and side paths around them (`resources.py`: the assembly, who keeps the table, church government).

## Build-ins

A click adds more of the same kind of thing:
- a list of parallel items (e.g. Spirit / Scripture / Reason; "Not… Not… Christ itself"), or
- the next part of one sentence or question (e.g. "We speak… / …where the Bible speaks"; a question that keeps extending).

Never build a claim, a quotation, and a punchline onto one slide. Different kinds of statements get separate slides; supporting remarks go in the notes.
